# AUDIT — R18, power of the Ljung–Box test on a binary stream

This is the only document transmitted to the orchestrator. It contains: what R18 measures and
what it does not; the five controls with their margins in standard errors and their trigger
probability under their own null; the deviation classification; the reproducibility evidence,
including the full `pytest tests/ -v` output pasted as-is and the SHA-256 digests of four runs;
the design decisions taken outside the plan; and the open questions, left open.

**R18 reproduces no figure, table or number of `articleB_whitening_v87.tex`.** It has no
originating script and no witness CSV. It measures the sensitivity of the instrument on which
four statements of the manuscript rest, and its output is a bound.

---

## 1. What R18 establishes, in one paragraph

At the configuration behind Figure 6 and L278 — `n = 8000`, lag 20, nominal level 5% — the
Ljung–Box test on a binary stream is correctly sized (`4.5%`, KS `p = 0.21` against
`Uniform(0,1)`) and attains 80% power against a lag-1 autocorrelation of **`rho_80 = 0.051`**
(measured `0.0506`, 95% cluster bootstrap `[0.0494, 0.0518]`; analytic root `0.05112`). Applied
to the streams the manuscript's non-rejections were computed on — 13,000 HoeffdingTree binary
error streams over R06's 13 penalties and 20,000 raw sign streams over R11's 20 — the largest
pooled lag-1 autocorrelation measured anywhere is **`0.000818`**, which is `1.6%` of `rho_80`
and at which the instrument's power is **`0.050`, its own level**.

**The four published non-rejections therefore exclude a lag-1 autocorrelation above `0.051` at
`n = 8000`, under a geometric-decay alternative, and exclude nothing below it.** The same
non-rejection is returned whether the true autocorrelation is `0.0008` or `0.04`. This neither
supports nor contradicts Proposition `prop:whitening`; it removes the reading under which a
non-rejection bounds the autocorrelation by something smaller than `rho_80`.

`theta_80` obeys an exact `n^{-1/2}` law because the non-centrality at 80% power is a constant of
the test — `20.9608` at every horizon, being the root of `P(chi2_nc(20, ncp) > 31.4104) = 0.80` —
and not a property of the sample:

| `n`       | `rho_80` measured | analytic  | 95% cluster bootstrap | interpolation bias | ratio to previous |
| --------- | ----------------- | --------- | --------------------- | ------------------ | ----------------- |
| `2,000`   | `0.1023`          | `0.10184` | `[0.0992, 0.1050]`    | `+0.01%`           | —                 |
| `8,000`   | `0.0506`          | `0.05112` | `[0.0494, 0.0518]`    | `+0.35%`           | `0.5020`          |
| `32,000`  | `0.0265`          | `0.02559` | `[0.0259, 0.0270]`    | `+0.51%`           | `0.5005`          |
| `128,000` | `0.0127`          | `0.01280` | `[0.0124, 0.0129]`    | `+0.47%`           | `0.5001`          |

## 2. Two things the reader must not take from this stream

**R18 validates nothing.** It measures an instrument against one family of alternatives,
`rho(k) = rho_1^k`, generated by a symmetric two-state Markov chain. A departure from whiteness
concentrated at a single high lag, or one that leaves the first 20 linear autocorrelations at
zero, has a different power curve and R18 does not measure it. The Ljung–Box statistic reads
nothing else, and neither does any other experiment in this repository.

**The bound is not uniform across the four sites.** L278, the Figure 6 caption (L286) and L290
run at `n = 8000`, where `rho_80 = 0.051`. L318's four ETF readings are **single** tests on
pre-2020 daily warm-up windows, whose lengths are bracketed by the two shortest horizons of the
sweep, so `rho_80` there lies between `0.051` and `0.102` — roughly twice as blunt, read once per
asset rather than as a rate over 1,000 streams.

## 3. Controls, with their margins and their trigger probabilities

### C1 — size at `theta = 0`, four horizons

The standard error of a proportion at the nominal level over 1,000 streams is
`sqrt(0.05*0.95/1000) = 0.006892`.

| `n`       | rate    | margin        | Wilson           | KS distance | KS `p` |
| --------- | ------- | ------------- | ---------------- | ----------- | ------ |
| `2,000`   | `0.051` | **+0.15 SE**  | `[3.90, 6.64]%`  | `0.0223`    | `0.69` |
| `8,000`   | `0.045` | **−0.73 SE**  | `[3.38, 5.97]%`  | `0.0333`    | `0.21` |
| `32,000`  | `0.052` | **+0.29 SE**  | `[3.99, 6.76]%`  | `0.0286`    | `0.38` |
| `128,000` | `0.047` | **−0.44 SE**  | `[3.55, 6.19]%`  | `0.0207`    | `0.77` |

All four cover 5%. Every KS distance is below the 95% Kolmogorov point `1.3581/sqrt(1000) =
0.04295`, and the `max`-KS across the four horizons sits at bootstrap `p = 0.5665`.

**Trigger probability under its own null.** Four simultaneous 95% Wilson gates miss at least once
with probability `1 - 0.95^4 = 18.55%`, above the 5% threshold of preamble §S4bis, which is why
the binary gate was replaced by the KS calibration test before any number was read. Each KS test
carries 5%; the `max`-KS bootstrap gate carries 5% by construction. The four rates are retained
as description and are not a criterion.

**Amendment A1 is answered.** The plan reported three rates and three KS p-values for a design
with four horizons. Four of each are computed, logged, and persisted to `R18_size_at_null.csv`, one row per horizon, none dropped.

### C2 — monotonicity in `theta` at fixed `n`, and in `n` at fixed `theta`

**Margin: 2 standard errors of the *paired* difference**, which is the ordinary two-sided 95%
separation of a paired mean and is derived from the sampling distribution of that difference, not
from any observed value. The pairing is not optional here: the grid and the horizons share their
streams by construction (C4), so an unpaired standard error would be the wrong denominator.

| direction               | adjacent comparisons | inversions found | largest, in paired SE | above the margin |
| ----------------------- | -------------------- | ---------------- | --------------------- | ---------------- |
| in `theta` at fixed `n` | 140                  | 3                | **1.50 SE**           | **0**            |
| in `n` at fixed `theta` | 108                  | 2                | **0.63 SE**           | **0**            |

The largest inversion is logged whether or not it reaches the margin, so that an absent inversion
and a small one stay distinguishable in the log.

**Trigger probability under its own null.** This control has no controlled family error and is
not a gate: a 2-SE criterion applied to 248 adjacent comparisons would fire with probability
indistinguishable from 1 if every adjacent pair were exactly equal. It is a **characterisation
threshold**, and the plan fixes its conduct accordingly — an inversion above the margin is
characterised and reported, never corrected. None reached it on this draw.

### C3 — empirical against analytic power

**Margin: `3 * 0.5/sqrt(1000) = 0.04743`**, three standard errors of a proportion at its worst
variance, applied on the domain `power_analytic < 0.95` and **not** applied above it, where the
local chi-square limit is not justified. The derivation is reproduced as a code comment.

- Worst deviation inside the domain: **`0.0421` = 2.66 SE**, at `n = 2000`, `theta = 0.025`,
  i.e. `0.888` of the budget.
- Over the whole grid, saturated region included: `0.0421`, the same point.

**Trigger probability under its own null: 16.6%, and this is a defect of the control as the
prompt specifies it.** A 3-SE per-point band applied as a maximum over the 67 amplitudes of the
domain fires with probability `1 - (1 - 0.0027)^67 = 0.1657` when the two curves agree exactly.
Preamble §S4.7 requires that this be said rather than the draw touched: **the control is
mis-sized, not the measurement.** The evidence is quantitative — the expected maximum of 67
independent standard normals is `2.61` and the observed maximum is `2.66`, so this draw sits at
the median of what a correct implementation produces. A `max`-statistic control of this kind
needs its own null distribution (as C1's `max`-KS has one), and the prompt's fixed 3-SE band does
not supply it. **No tolerance was widened and no draw was reselected**; the band is the prompt's
and it is applied as written.

**The deviation has a sign, and it is reported rather than absorbed.** The empirical rate exceeds
the analytic prediction at **48 of the 67** amplitudes inside the domain (sign test
`p = 5.2e-4` under independence, which the C4 pairing violates, so the p-value understates). The
non-central chi-square is a local limit and is not exact on a binary stream at these horizons.
No term was added to the prediction to absorb the difference.

### C4 — common random numbers

**Not a gate.** The design effect is measured and declared *before* any statement pooled over the
grid, and no such statement is published.

| quantity                                                                | value                            |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Kish design effect at `n = 8000`, 36,000 readings                       | **1.962**                        |
| effective sample size                                                   | **18,351** of 36,000             |
| cluster standard error / simple-random-sample standard error            | `0.00362 / 0.00258` = **1.401**  |
| mean correlation of the rejection indicator between two amplitudes      | `0.350` (max `0.868`)            |

`sqrt(1.962) = 1.401` reproduces the standard-error ratio exactly, which is the internal
consistency check on the two routes. The per-point Wilson intervals stay valid: each rests on
1,000 independent streams.

C4 is structural rather than declared. The seed key is `("R18", "power", index)` with no `theta`
and no `n_steps`, so one uniform vector of length 128,000 generates the chain at every amplitude
by re-thresholding and its first `n` entries generate every horizon. **The application arms
depart from C4 and the departure is declared**: their keys carry `gamma`, because R11's generator
draws the whole innovation vector before the variance recursion, so `sign(eps_t) = sign(z_t)` and
the sign stream would be bit-identical at all twenty penalties under a `Gamma`-free key. The arm
would then measure one number repeated twenty times.

### C5 — generator verification

**Margin: `4/sqrt(10000*8000) = 4.472e-4`**, four standard errors of a sample autocorrelation on
`N` observations, `1/sqrt(N)`, matched to the pooled estimator's `N = 8e7` within-stream pairs.

- Pooled lag-1 autocorrelation at `theta = 0.10`: **`0.19996861`** against the exact `0.20`,
  deviation **`−3.139e-5` = 0.28 SE**, i.e. `7.0%` of the budget.
- Marginal rate **`0.49992356`**, Wilson `[0.499814, 0.500033]` on 80,000,000 observations, which
  covers `0.5`. After the `sqrt((1+rho)/(1-rho)) = 1.2247` inflation the chain's own dependence
  imposes, `[0.499789, 0.500058]` — reported beside the prescribed interval, which is the
  criterion as written.

**Trigger probability under its own null:** `6.33e-5` two-sided for the autocorrelation arm,
`5%` for the marginal arm. Both are single tests.

### The instrument itself, cross-checked

Not one of C1–C5, and logged before any of them: `lb_pvalue` is carried byte-identically from
`exp_R02_whitening_ljungbox.py` and cross-checked against `statsmodels.stats.diagnostic.acorr_ljungbox` on 150 streams spanning three amplitudes and two
horizons. **Worst disagreement: `1e-4` of its own budget**, where the budget is
`chi2.pdf(q) * q * 1e-9` — the float64 reassociation error of a sum over at most `n` terms,
propagated to the p-value scale through the chi-square density. Eight primitives in total are
asserted byte-identical to the experiments that own them (3,628 characters compared), and the 21
statements carried inline from R02's `simulate_task` are each checked for presence in that
function.

**The degenerate branch of `lb_pvalue` fired 0 times** over 144,000 power readings and 33,000
application readings. It is counted and logged at zero because an absent counter and a zero
counter do not look different in a log; a non-zero count stops the run. `AUDIT_R06.md` §2.3 names
this branch as biased towards "white".

## 7. Design decisions taken outside the plan

**7.1 The figure is `figA05`, not the prompt's `figA04`.** Prompt §3 justifies `figA04` by
enumerating R02b, R02c and R04b as the holders of `figA01`–`figA03`. **R11 already emits
`results/R11_multi_detector/figures/figA04_adwin_blind_zone.png`** (`exp_R11_multi_detector.py:2597`); the enumeration missed it. Emitting a second `figA04` would
have put two different figures under one appendix number in a repository that numbers figures as
v87 renders them. The premise is stale, not wrong at the time it was written — R11 closed after
the prompt was drafted.

**7.2 The macros are cardinal, `\REighteen…`, not the prompt's ordinal `\REighteenth…`.**
Preamble §S6 fixes `\R<Ordinal><ClaimInCamelCase>` with "l'ordinal en toutes lettres anglaises
(`One`, `Two`, …, `Seventeen`)", and the preamble prevails over the prompt by its own precedence
clause. The repository realises cardinals throughout — `ROne` … `RSix`, `RTwoC`, and `REleven`,
which R11 emitted after correcting the identical defect in its own prompt and recorded in
`AUDIT_R11.md` §7. Emitting `\REighteenth…` would leave one stream out of eighteen with a
different namespace in a file destined for `\input`.

**7.3 Both application arms run at `n = 8000`**, including arm (b), whose generator and `Gamma`
grid come from R11 where `N_STEPS = 7000`. Prompt §1 imposes the instrument configuration
(`n_steps = 8000`, `lags = 20`, `n_streams = 1000`, level `0.05`) and R11 performs no Ljung–Box
test whose horizon could be inherited, so the arm borrows R11's *process* and runs it at the
instrument's horizon. `n_steps` is a column of the CSV, so the choice is visible rather than
implied. **This is a decision, and the alternative — running arm (b) at 7,000 and computing its
power at 7,000 — is defensible too.**

**7.4 The amplitude grid is a design decision.** The prompt gives only the domain `[0, 0.25]`.
The whole transition lives in `theta ∈ [0.006, 0.06]`, so a uniform grid on `[0, 0.25]` would put
`theta_80` inside the first cell at the large horizons and saturate everywhere else, and C2
requires one grid common to all four horizons. The grid is `{0}` ∪ 16 points per decade on
`[0.0025, 0.25]` (33 points) ∪ the anchors `{0.05, 0.10}` = **36 points**. `theta = 0.05` is on
the grid so the power at `rho = 0.10` is measured rather than interpolated. The interpolator's
own bias on that resolution is **measured, not assumed**: running it on the analytic curve over
the same grid gives at most `0.51%`, against a bootstrap half-width of `2.83%` in relative terms.

**7.5 The two arms carry two different `Gamma = 1` conventions, and both are visible in the
columns.** Arm (a) uses R06's grid, where `Gamma = 1` is `alpha = beta = 0`, a genuinely i.i.d.
stream. Arm (b) uses R11's, whose first *target* `1.0` lies below the attainable floor
`1 + 2*alpha/(1-alpha) = 1.1739130435` at `alpha = 0.08`, so the solver collapses to `beta = 0`
and the point runs at the floor as an ARCH(1) process. `Gamma_target`, `Gamma_realised` and
`attainable` are three distinct columns per amendment A3, and the finding itself is
`docs/DEVIATIONS.md` `R11-gamma-grid-floor`, cited rather than restated.

**7.6 `power_at_measured_rho` maps a measured `rho_1` onto the alternative that has that
`rho_1`.** The analytic power needs `rho(k)` for `k = 1..20`; the measurement supplies `rho(1)`
alone. The column therefore reports the power at the point of the *Markov* family whose lag-1
autocorrelation equals the measured one, `theta = |rho_1| / 2`. It is a modelling choice, it is
the same family the whole power curve is built on, and it is stated here and in
`docs/sections/R18.md` rather than left implicit. The interval is propagated from the `rho`
interval with the evenness of the power in `rho` respected: an interval straddling zero has the
**level** as its lower bound on the power, not the power at the smaller endpoint.

**7.7 A fifth CSV, `R18_size_at_null.csv`, beyond the prompt's four.** Preamble S4bis item 3
requires the `m` individual p-values of a multiple-test control to be kept "in the diagnostic CSV
descriptively". C1's four rates, four Wilson intervals, four KS statistics and
four KS p-values had no home in the prompt's schema, and folding them into `R18_power_vs_horizon.csv` would have mixed a diagnostic into a deliverable.

**7.8 Columns added beyond the prompt's schema.** `R18_detectable_amplitude.csv` carries
`rho_ci_low` / `rho_ci_high` (the interval on the interpretable quantity),
`ncp_at_theta_80_analytic`, `theta_80_grid_on_analytic`, `interpolation_bias` and
`ci_covers_analytic`. `R18_applied_to_sign_streams.csv` carries A2's `power_at_measured_rho` with
its propagated interval, plus the Ljung–Box rejection rate with its Wilson interval and the
median p-value. The prompt's columns are all present and in its order where it fixes one.

**7.9 The grid runs in two passes so that A4's cost risk is measured rather than estimated.** The
`n = 128,000` slice is run alone first and its cost logged before the other three horizons are
launched. It takes **128.0 s of the 291 s run, 44%**, and the design was not trimmed for it. The
plan's estimate of 10–15 minutes was pessimistic by a factor of three.

**7.10 The source-identity check is broader than any prompt requires.** Eight primitives are
asserted byte-identical to the three experiments that own them, and the 21 statements carried
inline from R02's `simulate_task` — which cannot be extracted as a function — are each checked
for presence in it. Two solvers collide in name (`solve_beta_for_gamma` exists in both R06 and
R11 and returns different betas for the same target), so each is carried under a suffixed name
and the check normalises exactly that one token on the `def` line. This follows R06's and R11's
doctrine; no prompt asked for it.

**7.11 The camera-ready trigger is evaluated in-script and logged**, not decided in prose after
the fact: `rho_80` against the largest measured `|rho_1|`, read from the arms themselves.

**7.12 C1's cross-horizon statement needed a null that the prompt does not supply.** The prompt
prescribes a KS test per horizon. Four nested horizons on shared streams give four dependent KS
statistics, so their maximum has no tabulated law; the null comes from a bootstrap resampling
**whole stream indices**, which carries the dependence, with each replicate scored against the
original empirical distribution of its own horizon.

## 8. Open questions, left open

1. **Which `theta_80` should a camera-ready sentence quote?** The grid estimate and the analytic
   root are two statements about one quantity. They agree at `n = 2000`, `8000` and `128,000`; at
   `n = 32,000` **the cluster-bootstrap interval on the grid estimate does not cover the analytic
   root** (`[0.012956, 0.013490]` against `0.012793`). Four intervals at 95% miss at least once
   with probability 18.5%, so the event is unremarkable in itself, and the parked candidate
   quotes `0.051` at `n = 8000` where the two agree. Which of the two the repository should
   publish as *the* bound is not decided here.

2. **C3's control is mis-sized as the prompt specifies it.** A 3-SE per-point band applied as a
   maximum over 67 amplitudes fires with probability 16.6% when the two curves agree exactly, and
   the observed maximum (2.66 SE) sits at the median of what a correct implementation produces
   (expected maximum 2.61 SE). Re-specifying it as a `max`-statistic with its own null — the
   treatment C1 received — would change a tolerance the prompt fixes, and that was not done.
   Whether the repository should re-cut it is for the assembler.

3. **What alternative should a power statement for a *whitening* claim use?** R18 measures power
   against `rho(k) = rho_1^k`, the family the symmetric two-state chain generates. A
   whitening claim is a statement about independence, and a dependence that leaves the first 20
   linear autocorrelations at zero is invisible to both the Ljung–Box test and this experiment.
   No test with power against non-linear dependence on a binary stream — McLeod–Li, BDS, a runs
   test — exists anywhere in this repository, and adding one is outside R18's remit.

4. **Should the application arm follow the instrument or the stream it borrows?** Arm (b) runs
   R11's process at the instrument's horizon (8,000) rather than R11's own (7,000). §7.3 states
   the reasoning; the opposite choice is defensible and the CSV carries `n_steps` either way.

5. **`R18_size_at_null.csv` is a fifth artefact where prompt §3 lists four.** Whether a
   diagnostic CSV belongs in the deliverable set, or should be folded into the log, is for the
   assembler.

6. **The Figure 6 caption is named and not bundled.** L278 and the caption at L286 report the
   *size* of the same test, not a non-rejection, and qualifying them means adding a power figure
   to a caption that currently carries a rate. That should be decided with whatever revision
   Figure 6 receives, and the parked candidate says so.

7. **The exact lengths of the four ETF warm-up windows are not read by R18.** The bound at L318 is
   stated as an interval (`0.051`–`0.102`) derived from the two horizons that bracket them. R01
   holds the per-asset day counts; reading them would sharpen the statement to a single number per
   asset. R18 does not depend on R01's artefacts and this was left as it stands rather than
   introduce the coupling.
