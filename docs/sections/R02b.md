### Stream Overview & Mathematical Target

R02b performs a dedicated sweep to dimension the i.i.d. arm over-rejection phenomenon reported in the manuscript. The experiment generates 1000 streams of length 8000 for each degrees-of-freedom value $\nu \in \{5, 6, 7, 8.5, 12, 30\}$ using Student's $t$ innovations and applies the Ljung--Box test (lag 20) to both raw and squared series. The theoretical target is the Ljung--Box validity condition: for a series $Y_t$, the test requires $E[Y_t^2] < \infty$; with $Y_t = \varepsilon_t^2$ this is $E[\varepsilon_t^4] < \infty$, i.e., $\nu > 4$.

### Execution & Verification

Deterministic execution is enforced via 128-bit seed digests, single-threaded BLAS confinement, and hash-seed pinning at 42. The pipeline checks seed uniqueness across all 6000 streams and confirms that the negative control (Ljung--Box on raw $\varepsilon_t$) holds the nominal 5% level at all grid points. Wilson 95% score confidence intervals are computed for all rejection rates. The experiment produces CSV data files, a figure (figA01_iid_overrejection_vs_nu.png), and LaTeX macro definitions (R02b_claims.tex).

### Output Artifacts

The primary outputs are: rejection rates and Wilson intervals for squared and raw streams across six $\nu$ values, a plot showing the transition in over-rejection behavior, and LaTeX macros for manuscript integration. The key finding is that the over-rejection phenomenon is real but occurs at $\nu \leq 6$, not $\nu = 7$ as stated. At $\nu = 7$, the rejection rate is 5.8% with Wilson [4.5, 7.4]%, which contains the nominal level, falsifying the manuscript's claim (D3 deviation).
